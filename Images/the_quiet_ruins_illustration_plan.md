# The Quiet Ruins — Illustration Plan

Visual direction throughout: gothic, dark, atmospheric fantasy in the style of the finalized cover—muted autumn colors, weathered stone, overcast or torchlit scenes, strong silhouettes, and restrained magical effects. Keep Mara visually consistent as an adult woman in a long coat with a backpack; when her face is visible, show only part of it where practical. Avoid making ambiguous voices or presences look definitively ghostly unless the chapter explicitly establishes a ghost.

## Chapter 1 — The Line North

1. **Mara on the northbound train:** Mara sits alone beside a rain-streaked window, her notebook open on her knee and the dark landscape sliding past. Her reflection overlaps the passing fields, suggesting unease without showing a supernatural figure.
2. **The first view of Hogwarts:** From the railway approach, Mara stands small against a broad valley and sees the ruined, spired castle beyond the tracks. Overcast sky, autumn foliage, distant illuminated windows; a composition that echoes the cover while bringing the story closer to the castle.
3. **The carved name:** Close view of Mara’s gloved hand and notebook beside a weathered stone bearing the Hallowell carving. Keep the mark ambiguous and the castle blurred in the distance.

## Chapter 2 — The Last Station

1. **The signal hut:** Mara searches the roof-collapsed railway hut by torchlight. The crossed-out white line, rusted lever, broken cabinet, and dead phone make the abandoned place feel recently emptied.
2. **The warning and the bell:** Mara pauses in the hut doorway as twilight settles over the tracks; the handwritten “Do not answer the bell” note is visible in her hand. The hills beyond are empty, though the scene feels as if a sound has just come from them.
3. **The injured hippogriff:** A frightened, wounded hippogriff stands tense on the slope, wings half-raised and three parallel grooves visible among its injuries. Mara keeps her distance, one hand near her pack, with the railway cutting and darkening sky behind them.
4. **The house-elf’s clue:** A brief, uneasy encounter in the ruined railway landscape: the strange house-elf offers or leaves a tangible clue connected to Snape, while Mara watches warily. Keep the elf unsettling and guarded, not cute or comic.

## Chapter 3 — The House at the Edge

1. **The sealed cottage:** Mara approaches the split, roof-collapsed cottage beside the rowan trees. The boarded windows and the white circle cut through by three strokes stand out against damp stone.
2. **The children’s shoes:** From outside the loosened window board, show Mara’s torch beam falling on a neat line of small shoes inside the blackened room. Her face is partly visible in profile, caught between caution and curiosity.
3. **The red scarf and evacuation traces:** In the cottage interior, Mara discovers the scarf marked “ELSPETH” among children’s belongings and scraps of evacuation records. Use dust, rotting wood, and a small pool of torchlight; make the evidence poignant rather than graphic.
4. **The descent beneath the wall:** Mara lowers herself into the narrow opening beneath the castle wall, backpack scraping stone. The cottage and grey daylight recede above her as darkness closes around the entrance.

## Chapter 4 — The Underwall

1. **The cramped tunnel:** Mara squeezes sideways through a low stone passage, one hand on the wall and torch held ahead. The tunnel bends sharply and seems to turn back on itself.
2. **The unreachable workroom:** Through a break in the masonry, Mara glimpses the sealed remains of Snape’s workroom beyond a collapse—shelves, a desk, and scattered objects just out of reach. Do not reveal the room fully.
3. **The pale tunnel creature:** A pale, eyeless shape is barely visible around a distant bend, caught at the edge of Mara’s torchlight. Keep its anatomy indistinct and its presence uncertain.
4. **The broken threshold:** Mara faces a damaged threshold at the end of the tunnel, knife ready, while faint chalk or scratched marks vanish into the dark beyond. Her posture shows fear held in check by determination.

## Chapter 5 — The Mouth of the Castle

1. **The pool beneath the castle:** Mara stands at the edge of a black underground pool, torch reflected in water that seems too still. A suggestion of movement beneath the surface should remain subtle, not a clearly depicted monster.
2. **The voice in her mother’s voice:** Mara turns toward an empty passage as if someone has called her. The frame shows only dark stone and the tense angle of her face, conveying an imitation without depicting a speaker.
3. **The SERVICE COURT door:** Mara confronts the heavy service door, where “WELCOME BACK, MARA” appears in the light. Her hand hovers near the latch; the words should feel unsettling and personal.

## Chapter 6 — The Service Court

1. **Crossing into Hogwarts:** Mara steps through the service entrance into a ruined court, framed by cracked arches and remnants of everyday castle life. Her small figure and backpack contrast with the scale of the empty architecture.
2. **Children’s marks:** Close view of children’s chalk marks and names—especially Aveline—on a wall or door, with Mara’s notebook and pencil entering the frame as she records them.
3. **The M. Hallow carving:** Mara examines the “M. Hallow” carving on the established door, its letters worn into the surface. Use a tight composition on the carving and her partly lit, uncertain expression; it is an echo, not proof of kinship.
4. **The route toward the stores:** Mara studies a rough map fragment at a junction in the service spaces, with the shadowed way down toward the old stores visible behind her.

## Chapter 7 — The Old Stores

1. **The descent:** Mara moves down the steep, narrow stair into the old stores, holding the map fragment close to her torch. Damp grooves and rough stone seem to funnel her deeper.
2. **The child’s shoe and six-place clues:** Mara kneels beside a small, empty shoe in the corridor. In her notebook, a quick sketch of six places is partly visible, linking observation to growing suspicion.
3. **The ajar door:** At the end of the corridor, a door stands open by only a hand’s width beneath an interrupted-circle mark and three grooves. Mara pauses before crossing the threshold.
4. **The black basin:** Mara faces the dark water or substance in the lower basin chamber as it begins to move beyond its edge. A faint green flare from the serpent button makes it recoil; keep the creature’s form undefined.

## Chapter 8 — The Sixth Place

1. **The hidden passage:** Mara pushes aside the leaning iron rack in the service court and discovers the unmarked door with the interrupted-circle symbol and small A. Dusty canvas and hooks frame the entrance.
2. **The six-child room:** A low room holds six places, with one hook empty and the traces of six cups, stools, or blankets. Mara stands among them, small beneath the low ceiling, studying the room rather than immediately touching anything.
3. **The Aveline bell:** Mara lifts the brass bell tagged “Aveline” from its hidden place. The words “Aveline was not the sixth” are visible nearby; the bell catches a faint, cold glint.
4. **The cost of the button:** The serpent button gives a brief green pulse as the black substance reaches the room. A stool and cup are damaged, glass cuts Mara’s cheek, and she clutches the bell—showing both the cost and her choice to keep it.

## Chapter 9 — The Names We Keep

1. **The war-damaged records room:** Mara enters a staff records room scarred by blasts and collapse, with ransacked belongings and makeshift shelter remnants among the shelves.
2. **The ledger and Hallowell entry:** A close, over-the-shoulder view of Mara reading the damaged ledger. The Hallowell entry, six-person record, and blank or damaged place are visible, with her fingers carefully keeping the page open.
3. **The house-elf’s bargain:** The frightening, twisted house-elf faces Mara across the records-room clutter, brass key in one hand and ledger or service-stair key exchange between them. Keep the elf’s posture wary and transactional.
4. **The door and the distant voices:** Mara stands before a threatening doorway while unseen adult voices seem to draw her onward. Show the empty passage beyond, letting sound and shadow imply danger rather than adding visible speakers.

## Chapter 10 — The Service Stair

1. **The spiral stair:** Mara descends the narrow, square-spiral service stair, pack tight against the wall. Small slits in the stone reveal fragments of a bench, hooks, and a hanging coat.
2. **The three-groove handprint:** Mara studies the scrubbed grey handprint and the three interrupted grooves scratched beneath it. Her torchlight picks out the marks while the lower stair disappears into darkness.
3. **The bell in the passage:** The wrapped bell shifts against the sealed cylinder inside Mara’s pack. She freezes on the stairs and looks back over her shoulder, the narrow space amplifying the small sound.
4. **The unmistakable ghost:** The first clearly visible ghost appears on the service stair landing, pale and solid enough to distinguish from shadows. Mara faces it in alarm; the bell and the descending stair connect them visually.

## Chapter 11 — The Second Dose

1. **The healer appears:** In a dim chamber below the stair, the dark healer stands in the torchlight, exhausted and almost kind, with a stained cuff and a small vial. Mara keeps her distance.
2. **The second dose:** Close composition of Mara holding or confronting the dose, with the vial’s pale contents catching the light. Her expression shows both fear and desire; the darkened effect should be subtle, not monstrous.
3. **Hidden routes revealed:** Mara sees faint routes or lines emerge through the stone as a consequence of the dose. The paths glow weakly against the dark walls, leading her gaze onward.
4. **Aveline stayed behind:** Mara witnesses a fragmentary vision or trace of Aveline choosing to remain while another person escapes. Keep the image memory-like and incomplete, preserving the mystery rather than depicting the full explanation.

## Chapter 12 — The Open Door

1. **The infirmary approach:** Mara enters a damaged room leading toward the infirmary, where abandoned medical objects and cracked tiles sit beneath a low, oppressive ceiling.
2. **The serpent button pushes back the black:** Mara raises the button as the black substance advances. A narrow green flare forces it away, illuminating her tense face and the newly visible route.
3. **“SHE LEFT SOMEONE BEHIND”:** The message appears on a wall or record as Mara reads it, her expression hardening. Surround the words with traces of the six-child history, but do not reveal who was left behind.
4. **The cylinder and note:** Mara retrieves Snape’s silver-marked cylinder and note from a dangerous, flooded or collapsing space. One hand reaches for them while the other braces against stone; something important is visibly lost behind her.

## Chapter 13 — The First Record

1. **Searching for the First Record:** Mara examines a cramped record space, papers and marks arranged around the clue that Aveline was “the door.” The bell and Snape’s cylinder are present but kept distinct in the composition.
2. **Snape’s warning:** Mara reads Snape’s warning by torchlight, her face split between the page’s pale glow and surrounding darkness. The warning should suggest that the entity understands her desire for power.
3. **Leaving the bell behind:** Mara sets the bell down and turns away from it, choosing not to carry it farther. Frame the bell in the foreground and Mara moving toward the open passage behind it.
4. **Pulled through the door:** Despite resisting, Mara is dragged toward the Sixth Place entrance. Her boots skid across the stone and her hands reach for the frame as darkness opens beyond; end on motion and dread.

## Chapter 14 — The Name Underneath

1. **The six circles:** Mara lands inside the ceilingless Sixth Place, surrounded by six child-sized circles cut into the floor and filled with wet-looking black. The healer stands at the centre holding a narrow vial; the dark above seems endless.
2. **Aveline and Ruth:** Aveline and Ruth face each other across the room, with the six circles between them. Aveline’s sacrifice and Ruth’s refusal to forgive should read through their distance and expressions, not through melodramatic gestures.
3. **The Hallowell line:** Show Mara examining the family record or the fixed line connecting the wall to the centre of the room. Her hand, the Hallowell name, and the severable line form the focus, emphasizing that family history is not the same as consent.
4. **Snape’s diagram as a route out:** Mara studies the scorched, splitting diagram and identifies the low block as a wedge, with the stone above it to be lifted first. Her knife, injured hand, and the marked route make the escape clue legible.
5. **Cutting the line:** Mara cuts or severs the line as the castle reacts: the floor tilts, a crack opens, doors slam, the bell sounds as if underwater, and the black changes direction. Keep the action readable while making the room feel unstable.
6. **The scar and the fading mark:** After escaping, Mara examines the lasting thin silver scar on her hand or wrist as a black three-groove mark fades. Her face is partly visible, set with determination; the surrounding corridor offers no reassurance that the danger is gone.
7. **Walking north:** Final image: Mara walks north through the ruined landscape, pack on her shoulders, carrying the thimble and papers. The castle recedes behind her beneath an overcast sky; leave the faintest suggestion of the entity’s reach in a shadow or mark, without making the ending conclusive.
